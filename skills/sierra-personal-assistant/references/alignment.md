# Alignment, updates and commitments

Help the person align before consequential execution, provide timely visibility and close loops. Do not turn every small task into a meeting.

## Find an alignment opportunity

Look for decisions affecting another person's work, changed scope, dependencies, ownership ambiguity, deadline risk, reversed assumptions or a proposal being treated as agreed. Verify later context before surfacing a thread as open.

For each priority finding, provide:

1. **Situation and evidence:** what is happening, actual source/date and whether the decision is confirmed.
2. **Why align:** the dependency or uncertainty and its relevance to the growing edge.
3. **With whom:** the responsible role/person supported by the source; if unknown, ask instead of guessing.
4. **When:** a real deadline or “before implementation”; mark urgency as inferred if no deadline exists.
5. **Next step:** a short question, proposed approach or update. Include a draft only when it helps.

Suggest the lightest action that resolves uncertainty: a targeted message may be sufficient. Evidence of prior approval should reduce the need for another check. Respect the person's agreed authority; do not seek broad permission for unrelated work.

## Draft for review

A useful draft includes the intended outcome, proposed approach, impact/trade-off, specific input needed and next step. For example: “I propose keeping the current interface while changing the internal retry logic. This affects the failure-handling work. Is there a dependency I should account for before implementation?” Fictional example only.

For an end-of-day update, include what changed, relevant impact, blocker/question and next step. Never add the person's private feedback or say the message was generated because of a development concern.

## Follow commitments

Maintain a private minimal list if persistence was agreed: source, commitment, owner, due date when explicit, status and last reviewed time. Unknown dates remain unknown. Do not mark completion from a reminder alone. Validate resolved or superseded items before nudging again. Shared trackers and colleague messages remain unchanged until the concrete edit/send is approved.

Keep findings concise and avoid repeating already-acknowledged items unless their status, urgency or next step changed.
